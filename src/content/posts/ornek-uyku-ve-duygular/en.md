Sleep and emotions feed each other all the time, even when we do not notice it. After a night of poor sleep we find our patience thinner and small things upsetting. When our feelings run strong, falling asleep becomes harder. In this post, let us take a closer look at how the two relate.

## A tired mind, tender feelings

When we have not rested enough, our emotional reactions can be sharper. A remark hurts a bit more, a task looks a bit heavier. This is because a mind that has not rested has a harder time balancing feelings. It does not mean there is something wrong with you.

## How emotions affect sleep

A worried, sad or excited mind may experience the night not as a quiet place but as a stage where thoughts grow louder. When we lie down, the feelings we put off all day can fill our heads. That is why knowing the link between sleep and emotions helps us be more understanding of ourselves at night.

## Small habits that support sleep

The following are general suggestions; they may not give the same result for everyone.

- **Regular hours.** Try to go to bed and get up at similar times each day.
- **Give screens a break.** Put your phone aside for the last half hour before bed.
- **Cut back on evening caffeine.** Tea and coffee can delay sleep in some people.
- **Keep your bed for sleep.** If you can, avoid working in bed.
- **Write your thoughts down.** Emptying your mind into a notebook before bed can lighten it.

## Nights when sleep will not come

Being angry with yourself for not sleeping can make sleeplessness worse. If you have tried for about twenty minutes and it is not working, some people find it helps to get up, do something calm in dim light, and return to bed when sleepy.

## When is it good to get support?

If sleep problems go on for a long time or clearly make your days harder, consulting a health or mental health professional is a good step. Sleeplessness can have many different causes, and they are best looked at together.

## A small note for the morning

After a poor night you do not have to make the day perfect. Being a little softer with yourself in the morning, lightening your load and taking short breaks through the day can help. Getting out into daylight and a little gentle movement also suit many people. Struggling when you are tired is understandable, and it may be temporary.

This post is general information; it does not replace a personal assessment or a diagnosis.
