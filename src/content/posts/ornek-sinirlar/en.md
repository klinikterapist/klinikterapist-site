Healthy boundaries are how we stay close to others without losing ourselves in our relationships. Setting a boundary often looks like selfishness, but it is quite the opposite: it is a way of respecting both yourself and the person in front of you. In this post, let us look briefly at what boundaries are and how to begin.

## What exactly is a boundary?

A boundary is an invisible line that marks what we say yes and no to, what we can carry and what we need. A boundary is not about controlling someone else. It is about deciding our own behaviour and choices. Instead of "Do not do this", setting a boundary sounds like "When this happens, I will do that."

## Your boundaries may be under strain

The following situations may suggest that your boundaries need a second look:

- Saying no makes you feel guilty or bad.
- You cannot make time for yourself and keep rushing to meet other people's requests.
- Resentment or tiredness keeps building up.
- You keep what you want inside instead of saying it.

These are not a diagnosis, only clues worth thinking about.

## Small steps towards healthy boundaries

You can learn to set boundaries by starting small.

- **Ask yourself first.** "What am I feeling here, and what do I need?"
- **Be short and clear.** Long explanations are often unnecessary. "I will not be able to help this week" is enough.
- **Stay kind but firm.** You can keep your boundary without being harsh.
- **Try it in a small situation.** Practise first in a lower-stakes setting.

## What to do with guilt?

Feeling guilty after setting a boundary is very common. It does not necessarily mean you did something wrong; it may just be the early stage of a new habit. Meeting the feeling without anger, saying "I am getting used to this", can help.

## Other people's reactions

The people around you may not adjust to your new boundaries straight away. That does not show your boundary is wrong; people also need time to get used to it. If boundaries keep causing difficulty in your relationships, talking to a professional can help.

## Boundaries can change over time

Boundaries are not lines drawn once and fixed forever. As your circumstances, relationships and needs change, your boundaries may change too. Something you gave freely in one season may wear you out in another. Looking at your boundaries from time to time, and adjusting them when needed, is a natural part of taking care of yourself.
